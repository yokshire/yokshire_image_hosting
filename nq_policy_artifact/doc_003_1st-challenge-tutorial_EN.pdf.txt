QML encodes classical input data into quantum states.
Using a limited number of qubits, quantum circuits can 
represent data in a high

Through rotations and entanglement, quantum circuits can

Effective Dimension
Effective dimension describes how much of a model’s capacity is 
actually used
for learning. 
It is different from simply counting the number of parameters.
In some studies, quantum neural networks show high effective dimension even with a relatively small number of 
trainable parameters.
This suggests that a compact quantum circuit may still express non
-
trivial decision boundaries.
For real
-
world tabular data, such as survey responses or credit
-
rating records, a small quantum circuit can be 
used as a meaningful baseline model.
Convergence Performance
A quantum circuit is trained through an iterative hybrid process.
First, the input data is encoded into the quantum circuit. Then, the circuit is measured to produce a prediction.
After that, a classical optimizer updates the trainable angles to reduce the loss.
The loss curve shows how the model gradually improves over training iterations.
For beginners, the key point is simple: A quantum circuit can be trained in a familiar machine
-
learning workflow 
–
 prediction, loss calculation, parameter update, and evaluation.
